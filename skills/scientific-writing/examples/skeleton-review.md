# Skeleton: review, perspective or guidance paper

Three variants share one spine. Pick the variant, fill the brackets, delete the ">" guidance lines before delivery.

Target: [journal or "default"]
Variant: [narrative review | perspective | guidance with worked example | systematic review]

---

# [Topic]: [genre label such as "overview and guidance", "a systematic review"]
> or a descriptive noun phrase without a colon for a perspective.

## Abstract or Summary (150 to 250 words)

[Context in one sentence.] [The problem the paper addresses.] [What the paper does: scope, number of items reviewed, the taxonomy or criteria, "We propose" or "Here we identify".] [Main finding or core recommendation as counts of items, not data.] [Implication.]

> Systematic review: use the journal's structured headings; retrieved and included counts in the first Results sentence; databases and search month in Methods.

## Introduction (4 to 6 paragraphs)

[P1 to P2: define the central concept; if coining a term, give the acronym once in parentheses.]

[P3: the gap, opened concessively ("Although early efforts ...").]

[P4: aims as a numbered list of two to four items; scope limits and exclusions with reasons.]

[P5: roadmap: "We first define X. We then review Y against Z. Finally, we propose W."]

> A guidance paper may add one sentence previewing the bottom-line recommendation.
> A narrative review states here, or at the start of the evidence section, how items were found, the cut-off date, and that the search was not systematic.

## Methods or scope section

[Narrative or perspective: one paragraph of scope and search.]
[Guidance with worked example: a short section introducing the case study: clinical question, model, development and validation data with n and prevalence, CI method, code and data location.]
[Systematic review: registration and reporting checklist; eligibility with a bulleted exclusion list; search with databases, fields, date and the full string in Table 1 or the supplement; screening with number of reviewers, tool, calibration sample and agreement; charting form as a bullet list; synthesis; AI tools used and for which step; what was not done.]

## Framework (before any evidence)

[The taxonomy, the criteria or the organising list. Table 1 or Figure 1 holds it. Each class defined in one or two sentences, then the criteria that later sections will apply.]

> This list is the spine: reuse its items as the headings of the body, the panels of the evidence figure and the rows of the recommendation table.

## Body

> Choose one template and apply it identically to every item.

**Template A (perspective use cases):** [Scenario or category claim.] [How the system would work, with a hypothetical vignette.] ["A solution needs to ..." requirements.] [Prior evidence with citations.]

**Template B (guidance items):** [Definition.] [Interpretation.] [Critique against the criteria.] [Worked value from the case study with 95% CI and figure.] [Verdict in one short sentence.]

**Template C (evidence survey):** [Subsection per item of the organising list, alternating the classes compared; counts and sizes; the figure panel; one-sentence verdict against the framework.]

**Template D (systematic review Results):** [PRISMA flow with counts.] [One paragraph per figure in figure order; counts as n (%); means as mean (SD); past tense for findings, present for figure pointers; no "we".]

## Challenges and opportunities

[Each challenge (validation, verification, bias, privacy, cost, regulation) paired with a concrete mitigation in the same paragraph.]

> This section doubles as limitations in a perspective.

## Discussion

[What was done with counts.] [Core recommendation.] [Rebuttal of a common belief with the reason.] [Side topics as a numbered list.] [Scope limitations with reasons.] [Recommendation table introduced here (guidance).] [Systematic review: separate Limitations section covering missed publications, reliance on self-reported metrics and the reviewers' own tooling.]

## Conclusion (200 to 300 words for a perspective; two sentences for guidance; two paragraphs for a systematic review)

[Short claim opening the paragraph.] [The hurdles.] [Call to a named community or the next step.]

> Forecasts hedged with "likely", "could", "probably"; counts unhedged.

## Back matter

**Data availability** ["No new data were generated" or the OSF record and supplementary files.]
**Code availability** [Screening tool or analysis code with URL and licence, or a statement that none was produced.]
**Author contributions, Funding, Competing interests, Acknowledgements** [Journal format; AI-use disclosure naming tool and task; disclose if an LLM generated figures or code.]
