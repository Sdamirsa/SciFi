# Captions

Figure legends and table titles. Across the corpus, caption titles were descriptive labels in 13 of 14 papers; claim titles appeared only in two Nature-portfolio figures. Length varied tenfold within single papers (32 to 320 words), and nine papers had captions that could not be read without the main text. The rules below fix both.

## 1. Structure

A figure caption has four parts, in this order.

1. **Title.** A short descriptive noun phrase of 3 to 12 words, set in bold where the journal does so, ending with a full stop. It names what is shown, not what it means: "Kaplan-Meier curves stratified by body composition measures." "Model performance across tasks and user settings." "Overview of the agent pipeline." Two figures must not share near-identical titles.
2. **Panel descriptions.** One clause or sentence per panel, led by the panel letter in the journal's case ("a, ..." or "(A) ..."), in panel order. Each states what is plotted, on which data, with which groups or conditions. For a schematic, walk the stages in order.
3. **Reading notes.** Everything a reader needs to interpret the marks without the text: n per group and per panel; what bar height, points and lines represent; what error bars or bands are (SD, SE, 95% CI) and how they were obtained (bootstrap with resamples, exact binomial); the test behind each p value, its sidedness and any multiplicity correction; cut-offs or thresholds per panel; the adjustment set for adjusted effects; the sort order of rows; the colour or symbol legend when the figure relies on it; reference lines; the data source or sampling (top five patients per calculator; one rater per item).
4. **Abbreviations and credits.** Every abbreviation used on the figure or in the caption, defined again here even if defined in the text, as an alphabetised list. Source and licence for any reused image. Nature-portfolio adds "Source data are provided as a Source Data file."

A table has a title (part 1 only) and a footnote block that carries parts 3 and 4.

## 2. Length

- Schematic or overview figure: 40 to 120 words.
- Single-panel result figure: 40 to 80 words.
- Multi-panel result figure: 80 to 200 words. Above 230 words the caption is doing the work of a Methods paragraph; cut it and point to Methods.
- Table title: 5 to 14 words. Table footnotes: as long as needed, in short sentences.
- The target caption carries the statistics once; it does not repeat the entire statistical analysis paragraph of Methods (one Nature paper did).

## 3. Tone

- Titles are labels. The finding belongs in the Results text. Exception: Nature and Nature Cancer permit a one-sentence finding in the caption body after the panel descriptions ("Med-PaLM narrows the gap with clinicians"); write it as a plain statement with the numbers already on the figure, never as praise.
- No "note that", "it can be seen that", "interestingly", "clearly".
- Present tense throughout ("a, Accuracy of each model on...").
- A central illustration or take-home figure may carry a short narrative caption of the mechanism; keep claims hedged.

## 4. Stand-alone test

A caption passes if a reader who sees only the figure and the caption can answer: what is plotted, for whom (n), under what conditions, what the marks and colours mean, what the error bars are, what test produced the p values, and what the abbreviations stand for. Nine corpus papers fail on at least two of these.

## 5. Formats by journal

| Journal | Caption lead-in | Panel letters | Abbreviations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nature, Nature Cancer, Nat Comms, npj | "Fig. N \| Bold title." then panel text with bold lower-case letters | a, b, c | Not redefined by convention, but do it anyway in the final clause |
| Lancet Digital Health | "Figure N: Title" then text | (A), (B) | Redefine all; the Lancet family requires it |
| JAMIA | "Figure N. Title." then text | (A), (B) | Redefine all |
| EHJ | Bold coloured "Figure N" label, title sentence, panel paragraph, then an alphabetised abbreviation list separated by semicolons; the test used per panel at the end | A to F in bold | Redefine all |

Supplementary captions follow the same four-part structure; a template caption repeated with one word changed ("Classification matrix presenting [metric] of five models") is acceptable for parallel supplementary figures.

## 6. Checks

- Caption text matches the figure type and the text claims that cite it (no "schematic" caption on a data plot).
- Panel letters in caption, figure and text agree in case and order.
- Every number in a caption also appears, identically, in the Results text or the table it summarises.
- The caption never introduces a result that the text does not report.
- Medians in the plot and means in the text: say so in the caption, or better, align them.
- No colons in the caption body except after a panel letter where the journal style uses one.
