# Title

## Forms seen in the corpus

| Form | Count | Example shape (paraphrased) | When it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| Descriptive noun phrase, no colon | 7 | Development and validation of [system] for [task] in [domain] | Default for original research in JAMIA, EHJ, npj |
| Descriptive phrase plus colon and design | 3 | [Method] from [data] for [outcome]: a multicentre study | Lancet family requires the design after the colon |
| Coined name plus colon and description | 2 | [NAME]: a multimodal benchmark for [target] | Resource or system papers in Nature-portfolio |
| Declarative claim | 2 | Large language models encode clinical knowledge | Nature Articles only, and only when the finding is unambiguous |

Lengths ran 6 to 20 words. Aim for 10 to 16. A 20 word title names method, scope, data, outcome and design and reads as a sentence; cut the scope words first.

## Rules

- Name the method or system, the clinical task or outcome, and (for clinical studies) the design or data. Population and setting go in when they are the point ("in patients with..." or "a multicentre study").
- Prefer "for mortality prediction" over "enhances mortality prediction". The Lancet paper in the corpus had a claim verb in its preprint title and a neutral phrase in print; the editors removed the claim.
- No abbreviations in the title except ones more familiar than their expansion (CT, MRI, AI is borderline; LLM is not yet). "AI-based" is accepted in Lancet Digital Health titles.
- No "novel", "first", "towards" (unless the journal and the series branding call for it), no metaphor ("shaky foundations" is an npj review, not a research title).
- A colon separates topic from design or genre ("overview and guidance", "a systematic review", "a multicentre study"). Do not use a colon to join two descriptive phrases.
- Running head: a 6 to 8 word descriptive phrase derived from the title, often required by EHJ and JAMIA.

## In a review

A descriptive noun phrase naming the technology and the target concept ("Foundation models for generalist medical artificial intelligence"), or topic plus colon plus genre ("...: overview and guidance", "...: a large language model-assisted systematic review"). A coined term may appear if the paper introduces it.

## Checks

- Does the title promise exactly what the conclusion delivers, at the same strength?
- Would a reader searching for this work find it from the title's nouns alone?
- Is every word doing work? Remove "a study of", "an analysis of", "using artificial intelligence" when the method is already named.
