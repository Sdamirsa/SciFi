# Abstract

## Formats by journal

| Journal | Format | Headings | Length | Numbers expected |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| JAMIA | Structured | Objective(s); Materials and Methods; Results; Discussion; Conclusion (Discussion may be merged into Conclusion) | 250 | Sample sizes, headline metrics with CI |
| Lancet Digital Health | Structured | Background; Methods; Findings; Interpretation; Funding | 250 to 300 | n, follow-up, events, adjusted effects with 95% CI and p |
| European Heart Journal | Structured | Background (or Aims); Methods and results; Conclusion(s); then Keywords | 250 to 350 | Headline metric with CI, incremental value with p |
| npj Digital Medicine | Unstructured, one paragraph | none | 150 to 200 | At least one head-to-head number; sample size |
| Nature, Nature Cancer, Nature Communications | Unstructured, one paragraph | none | 150 to 200 (Nature up to 250) | Design numbers and the headline comparison |

## The move sequence (all formats)

1. Background: one or two sentences. The clinical problem or the field state, then the specific limitation of current practice or prior work. Open the limitation sentence with "However" only once in the whole abstract.
2. Aim: one sentence. "We aimed to..." or "We developed and validated...". For a method paper, "Here we introduce..." (Nature-portfolio) or "We present..." (others).
3. Methods: design, data source and period, n for development and test sets, reference standard, primary outcome or metric, comparator. Name the sites if multicentre. One to three sentences.
4. Results: the headline number with its uncertainty first, then one or two secondary numbers, then one efficiency or feasibility number if relevant (seconds per report, runtime). Pair every comparison as "X versus Y" with the same metric and decimals. Three to five sentences; in structured abstracts this is the longest part.
5. Conclusion: one or two sentences restating the finding at the strength the design supports, plus the practical implication. Lancet calls this Interpretation and keeps it to one sentence. No new information, no forecast that the Results do not support, no "may revolutionise".

## Rules

- Define each abbreviation at first use in the abstract, and define it again in the main text. The abstract is read alone.
- No citations in the abstract (Nature tolerates them; no one else does).
- No numbers that do not appear identically in the main text.
- Match the abstract's conclusion strength to the Discussion. The corpus includes abstracts claiming "near-perfect performance" and "a robust foundation for deployment" while the Discussion calls the work preliminary; this is the mismatch reviewers quote back.
- For structured abstracts, use the journal's headings verbatim, including the plural or singular form.
- Spell out one number sentence completely, for example "accuracy 92.4% (95% CI 89.1 to 94.9) versus 61.0% (56.2 to 65.6)".

## Companion panels

**Research in context (Lancet family, research articles only).** Three subheadings, each a short paragraph.
- Evidence before this study: the databases searched, the search terms, the date, the number of hits, and the gap stated as what prior work lacked (for example "three or fewer tissues, none from attenuation scans"). 100 to 200 words.
- Added value of this study: two or three sentences stating what is new and the key number.
- Implications of all the available evidence: two sentences, clinically oriented, hedged.

**Translational perspective (EHJ).** About five sentences moving from the known mechanism to the new tool, what it detects, its incremental value, and what it changes in practice. Sits after the abstract and keywords.

**Key points or highlights (some journals).** Three bullets of under 20 words each: what was known, what this adds, what it means.

## In a review

Unstructured, 150 to 250 words, five moves: context in one sentence, the problem the review addresses, what the review does (scope, number of items reviewed, the taxonomy or criteria), the main finding or the core recommendation (as counts of items that meet criteria, not as data), and the implication. For a systematic review use the journal's structured headings and give retrieved and included counts in the first Results sentence, with the databases and the search month and year in Methods. The guidance paper in the corpus states its recommendation in the abstract, at the end of the Introduction, in the Discussion and in the Conclusion; that repetition is deliberate and effective for guidance.
