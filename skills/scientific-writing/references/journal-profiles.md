# Journal profiles

What changes when a target journal is named. Everything not listed here follows the global rules and the balanced default in SKILL.md. Confirm current author guidelines before submission; these profiles reflect the corpus papers (2019 to 2026) and may lag.

## How to apply a profile

Look for the journal in the request, in a header line of the manuscript ("Target: npj Digital Medicine"), or in the file name. Apply the profile silently and mention it in one line of the reply. If the user later names a different journal, list the structural changes needed (Methods position, abstract format, heading style, caption format, back matter order) before rewriting anything.

## npj Digital Medicine

- Article type "Article". Unheaded Introduction ending with "Here we..." or "In this study, we..." and, optionally, a short numbered contributions list. Results before Discussion before Methods.
- Unstructured abstract, about 150 to 200 words, at least one numeric result expected (several corpus abstracts had none and read as weak).
- Results subheadings: labels or question-style headings for ablations; claim headings are accepted. One or two setup sentences per Results subsection because Methods comes last. Interpretation inside Results is tolerated but keep it to one closing sentence per subsection.
- Figures: "Fig. N | Title." captions with lower-case bold panel letters; "Fig. 2b" in text. Display items can be many (one corpus paper had 14); aim for 6 to 8.
- Back matter: Data availability, Code availability (named licence, controlled-access route for MIMIC-derived data), References, Acknowledgements (funding inside, with a funder-role sentence), Author contributions (free text by initials), Competing interests, Additional information. Reporting summary is Nature-portfolio style.
- Known tolerance for promotional wording in corpus papers; do not use it.
- British spelling with -ize, no serial comma.

## JAMIA (Journal of the American Medical Informatics Association)

- Structured abstract with headings Objective(s), Materials and Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusion (Discussion may be merged into Conclusion). About 250 words.
- Full IMRaD with headings "Introduction", "Materials and Methods" (or "Methods"), "Results", "Discussion", "Conclusion"; a "Limitations" subheading or separate section is accepted. Methods before Results.
- Results strictly descriptive; label subheadings matching Methods.
- Captions "Figure N." with upper-case (A), (B) panels and all abbreviations defined; tables with an abbreviation footnote.
- Back matter order: Author contributions (CRediT roles by initials), Supplementary material, Funding (with NIH disclaimer where relevant), Conflicts of interest, Data availability, Acknowledgements (AI-use disclosure may sit here, naming the tool and the task), References.
- Reference numbers in square brackets or superscript at sentence end.
- JAMIA accepted corpus papers with no CIs; do not rely on that.

## Nature, Nature Cancer, Nature Communications (Nature-portfolio research journals)

- Nature Article: unheaded Main (Introduction), then Results blocks without a "Results" heading, a Discussion, an optional separate Limitations section, a short Conclusion, then Methods after the references with its own reference numbering continuing from the main list, then Data availability, Code availability, Acknowledgements, Author contributions, Funding, Competing interests, Additional information, Extended Data, Reporting Summary.
- Nature Cancer and Nature Communications: same order with Methods last and a "Statistics and reproducibility" paragraph in Methods (sample size calculation, exclusions, randomisation, blinding, replicates, even when "none").
- Unstructured abstract 150 to 250 words; a one-sentence editorial summary may be requested.
- Introduction ends with explicitly enumerated contributions ("Our first key contribution...") or a bulleted list mapped to figures, and previews headline numbers.
- Results subheadings as claim sentences are the norm for clean findings; bold run-in claim headings inside subsections are used in Nature. Verdict sentences and hedged explanations inside Results are accepted.
- Captions "Fig. N | Bold title." with bold lower-case panel letters; Extended Data figures and tables carry full captions; "Source data are provided as a Source Data file." where applicable.
- Few or no main-text tables; Extended Data tables hold dataset summaries, rubrics, leaderboards, examples. Clinical cohort papers should still include a Table 1.
- Author contributions in free text by initials grouped by activity; competing interests list employment and stock by initials.
- British spelling with -ize, no serial comma, "For example" preferred over "such as", "ref. N" when a citation follows a number.
- Citations in the abstract are permitted here only.
- Promotional wording is tolerated by editors; the house rule still bans it.

## The Lancet Digital Health

- Structured abstract: Background, Methods, Findings, Interpretation, Funding; 250 to 300 words; Findings carry n, follow-up, events and adjusted effects with 95% CI and p.
- "Research in context" panel for research articles: Evidence before this study (databases, terms, date, hits, gap), Added value of this study, Implications of all the available evidence.
- Headings: Introduction, Methods (subsections including "Statistical analysis" and ending with "Role of the funding source"), Results, Discussion. No Conclusion heading; the Discussion ends with "In conclusion,". One limitations paragraph, second to last, opening "Our study has several limitations".
- Results strictly descriptive, label subheadings, every effect as "adjusted HR 2·39 (95% CI 1·92 to 2·96; p<0·0001)" in print; write "2.39 (95% CI 1.92 to 2.96), p<0.0001" in the manuscript and let the typesetter convert. Ranges with "to". p values to four decimals or "p<0·0001".
- Figures "Figure N: Title" with (A), (B) panels; captions redefine all abbreviations and state cut-offs and adjustment sets.
- Back matter: Contributors (narrative by initials; CRediT accepted), Declaration of interests, Data sharing, Acknowledgements, References. Ethics and consent in the first Methods subsection with the Declaration of Helsinki.
- Reviews (Health Policy, Review, Personal View): no Research in context panel; "Panel" instead of "Box"; numbered sections are removed in print.
- British spelling with -ise; numerals for all numbers with units.

## European Heart Journal

- Structured abstract: Background (or Aims), Methods and results (one combined section, organised "In Study 1... In Study 2..." for multi-study papers), Conclusion(s); then Keywords and a "Translational perspective" box of about five sentences.
- Headings: Introduction, Methods (with subsections; ethics and consent in the first "Study design" subsection; "Statistical analysis" last), Results (subheadings tagged by study), Discussion (unheaded opening paragraph, then thematic subheadings, "Limitations" as the last subheading), Conclusions as a separate short section.
- Effects as "adjusted HR 1.12 (95% CI 1.08 to 1.15), P < 0.001" with capital P; counts as "1391 (82.0%)".
- Figures in shaded boxes with bold coloured "Figure N" labels; captions with a title sentence, panel paragraph with (A) to (F), the test per panel, then an alphabetised abbreviation list separated by semicolons. A "Take home figure" (central illustration) is standard.
- Supplementary pointers use the fixed phrase "Supplementary material online, Table S2".
- Back matter: Supplementary material statement, Acknowledgements, Funding, Conflict of interest (patents and spin-outs in detail), Data availability, References.
- British spelling with -ize (remodelling, characterized).
- Avoid the promotional register of the corpus EHJ paper ("striking", "state-of-the-art", "novel" in the title).

## Quick conversion table

| Item | Balanced default | Nature-portfolio | Lancet DH | JAMIA | EHJ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Methods position | before Results | after Discussion | before | before | before |
| Abstract | structured, 250 | unstructured, 150 to 250 | structured BMFI + Funding | structured OMRDC | structured BMC + perspective box |
| Results preview in Introduction | no | yes, contributions list | no | no | hypothesis only |
| Results subheadings | labels | claims allowed | labels | labels | labels tagged by study |
| Interpretation in Results | one closing sentence | verdict sentence per subsection | none | none | minimal |
| Limitations | one paragraph | separate section allowed | one paragraph | subsection or section | last Discussion subheading |
| Conclusion heading | optional | short section | "In conclusion," in Discussion | section | separate section |
| Panel letters | a, b | a, b | (A), (B) | (A), (B) | (A), (B) |
| Caption lead | "Figure N." | "Fig. N \|" | "Figure N:" | "Figure N." | "Figure N" label |
| Author contributions | CRediT | free text by initials | narrative or CRediT | CRediT | not always required |
| Spelling | British -ise | British -ize | British -ise | either | British -ize |
