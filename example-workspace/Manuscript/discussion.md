# Discussion

In this study, we compared an open-weight LLM run on hospital hardware with a rule-based system for extracting valvular heart disease severity from 1,000 echocardiography test reports at two Swiss centres. The LLM reached an accuracy of 96.3% (95% CI 95.1 to 97.4) against 88.7% (87.0 to 90.4) for the rule-based system. Accuracy at centre B, whose reports played no part in prompt development, was 95.8% (93.9 to 97.5). Most remaining errors arose in reports that graded a lesion between two categories. Together, these results indicate that a locally deployed model can extract severity grades at a level close to cardiologist labelling without sending reports outside the hospital.

The advantage over rule-based extraction agrees with earlier reports on the limits of hand-written rules. A rule-based system for echocardiography reports reached 97.1% precision at its development hospital but 81.4% accuracy on reports with intermediate grades [@lindqvist2022rules]. A proprietary LLM in a single-centre study reached 93.5% accuracy, but it required reports to be sent to an external service [@mahmoudi2025echo]. Our model reached a higher accuracy while running entirely inside the hospital network, although the two studies used different label sets and report languages. These results suggest that a locally deployed open-weight model is a powerful alternative to rule-based extraction for registries that cannot send data outside the hospital.

The second question was whether accuracy would hold at a centre whose reports played no part in prompt development. Prompts were written and revised over three rounds on 200 reports from centre A, and reports from centre B were seen only at test time. At centre B the model reached an accuracy of 95.4% (95% CI 93.9 to 97.5), which lies within the interval of the overall estimate. Clinical text models often lose accuracy when moved between hospitals because templates, abbreviations and grading habits differ [@vandermeer2024external]. Our result confirms that an open-weight model prompted without fine-tuning generalises across Swiss university hospitals. Both centres write their reports in German and use the same reporting software.

Errors concentrated in reports that graded a lesion between two categories, such as mild to moderate regurgitation. A possible explanation is that the prompt asked for one of four grades, so the model had to choose a side when the report did not. Cardiologists resolve the same phrases inconsistently, and guideline grading leaves intermediate categories undefined for most lesions [@tanaka2025grading]. A rival explanation, that the model misread negated or historical findings, accounted for far fewer errors in our analysis. Allowing an intermediate grade in the output schema, or standardising the report template, may therefore reduce errors more than further prompt tuning would.

Our study has several limitations, and the most important concerns the reference standard. Both cardiologists worked at the two study centres, so shared local grading habits may have inflated agreement with the model. Second, both centres write reports in German within one health system, and we did not test other languages or reporting cultures. Third, we evaluated one open-weight model with one prompt, and a different model or prompt version could perform differently. Fourth, the design was retrospective, and we did not measure how automated extraction affects registry completeness or staff time in practice. A prospective evaluation within a registry workflow, at centres with different templates and languages, would address each of these limitations.

In conclusion, an open-weight LLM running inside the hospital network extracted valvular heart disease severity from echocardiography reports more accurately than a rule-based system, and its accuracy held at a second centre. Such a model could keep valve disease registries current without manual abstraction and without reports leaving the hospital. The model is not yet ready for registry use without human review. Prospective validation in a registry workflow, at centres with other languages and templates, should come before routine deployment, together with monitoring of accuracy after any change to report templates.
