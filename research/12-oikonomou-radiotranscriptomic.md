Oikonomou EK, Williams MC, Kotanidis CP, Desai MY, Marwan M, Antonopoulos AS, Thomas KE, Thomas S, Akoumianakis I, Fan LM, Kesavan S, Herdman L, Alashi A, Hutt Centeno E, Lyasheva M, Griffin BP, Flamm SD, Shirodaria C, Sabharwal N, Kelion A, Dweck MR, Van Beek EJR, Deanfield J, Hopewell JC, Neubauer S, Channon KM, Achenbach S, Newby DE, Antoniades C. A novel machine learning-derived radiotranscriptomic signature of perivascular fat improves cardiac risk prediction using coronary CT angiography. European Heart Journal 2019;40:3529-3543. doi:10.1093/eurheartj/ehz592

- Journal: European Heart Journal (section "Clinical Research", topic Coronary artery disease); open access, CC BY; accompanied by an editorial.
- Article type: original research (translational, three linked studies: biopsy radiotranscriptomics, nested case-control model development plus cohort testing in SCOT-HEART, and an AMI case-control with follow-up).
- Word count estimate: abstract about 360 words; main text about 4,500 words; 7 figures plus a Take home figure; 39 references; supplementary tables S1 to S12.
- Source: local PDF (publisher version downloaded from https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/40/43/3529/5554432), all 15 pages read.

# Global patterns

**Sentence length.** Sample of 20 sentences: range 9 to about 80 words, median about 30. Methods sentences are the longest (Study 2 design and Cox covariate sentences, 75 to 80 words). Paragraphs: Introduction 5 to 8 sentences (150 to 230 words), final Introduction paragraph a single sentence; Results paragraphs 3 to 6 sentences; Discussion paragraphs 4 to 9 sentences (130 to 250 words).

**Voice and person.** Mixed. "We" is used for the group's own prior discoveries in Introduction and Discussion ("We have recently demonstrated that", "We have recently discovered"), for the hypothesis, for design choices ("we designed a case-control study"), and to narrate steps in Results ("We next determined the FRP signature"). Methods are mostly passive ("biopsies were harvested", "Bonferroni correction was applied").

**Tense.** Introduction: present for established facts ("remains a leading cause"), present perfect for the group's earlier work, past for the hypothesis. Methods and Results: past. Results interpretation sentences switch to present ("This proof-of-concept analysis suggests"). Discussion opener and Conclusions: present ("we now demonstrate", "FRP detects persistent adverse structural remodelling").

**Hedging and certainty.** Hedges: suggest/suggesting, may, could, appeared to, may be needed. Strong verbs: demonstrate, confirm, reveal, significantly improves. Claims are mostly anchored to a figure call-out at sentence end ("(Figure 6A)") or a statistic in the same sentence.
**Punctuation.** Very heavy parentheses, including nested square brackets inside round brackets for statistics and definitions ("[ICC 0.938 (95% CI: ...), P < 0.001]"). Semicolons appear in prose lists of endpoints, in reference to supplementary items, and as separators in caption abbreviation lists. Colons are rare in prose, used in labels ("95% CI:"). Inline enumerations use roman numerals "(i) MACE; (ii) ...; (iii) ...". "e.g." sits inside parentheses. Serial comma inconsistent. Single quotation marks for coined terms ('radiome').

**Abbreviations.** Dense: more than 35 defined (CAD, CCTA, PVAT, FAI, FRP, MACE, HRP, CCS, ICC, HU, AMI, STEMI, RCA, LCA, AT, OXACCT, ROC, AUC, GPU, AI and study acronyms). Core terms (PVAT, FAI, CCTA, MACE, FRP) are defined in the abstract and again in the main text. Problems: AI is defined at its second appearance in the abstract and redefined in Limitations; CRISP-CT is expanded twice with different wording; AMI is redefined; GPU and OXACCT are used once or twice; C-statistic (abstract) and AUC (main text) are used for the same metric. Every figure caption ends with its own alphabetical abbreviation list.

**Numbers and statistics.** Pattern: estimate, 95% CI, P value, usually bracketed: "adj. HR 1.12 (95% CI 1.08 to 1.15), P < 0.001". Counts with percentages: "1391 (82.0%)". AUC given to 3 decimals in Results (0.774) and rounded to 2 in the abstract (0.77). CI label formatting varies ("95%CI:", "95% CI", "95% CI:"). Fold-change statements ("10.8-fold", "43-fold") appear in text without CI, with the CI only in the figure. Medians with interquartile ranges for follow-up. Thousands without separators in text (1575, 5487). Inconsistent "6 months" and "six months".

**Citation placement.** Numbered superscripts, mostly at sentence end, also mid-sentence right after a named study or method ("CRISP-CT study,16"). Usually 1 to 2 per claim, ranges for background clusters (12 to 15). Guidelines cited for context and definitions (ESC, ACCF/AHA, NICE, SCCT). Trial registration (NCT number) and ethics reference numbers inline. No reporting checklist (TRIPOD, STROBE, CLAIM) named.

**Transitions.** Openers: However (most frequent pivot), Furthermore, Of note, Notably, Importantly, Indeed, Finally, In contrast. Introduction moves clinical burden, then current test, then unmet need ("However, ..."), then the group's mechanism, then the unaddressed aspect ("However, ..."), then the enabling method, then hypothesis.

**Tone.** Noticeably promotional for a clinical journal: "striking improvement", "over and above the current state-of-the-art", "AI-powered", "novel" in title and conclusion, "promising approach", "critical validation study", plans for proprietary cloud software in Limitations. No "revolutionary" or "groundbreaking".

**Word choice.** High jargon density and nominalisations (phenotyping, quantification, stratification). British spelling with -ize (remodelling, characterized). Coined terms introduced in quotes, then used plainly.

# Local patterns by section

**Title.** Declarative claim, about 20 words, no colon. Names the method (machine learning-derived radiotranscriptomic signature), target tissue (perivascular fat), outcome (cardiac risk prediction) and modality (coronary CT angiography). No design or population. Running head shortens it to a descriptive phrase.

**Abstract.** ESC structured format: Background / Methods and results / Conclusion. Background (about 65 words) states the known mechanism, a "However" gap and the hypothesis. Methods and results (about 250 words) is organised as "In Study 1 ... In Study 2 ... In Study 3", fusing methods with numbers: n per study, 1391 features, C-statistic with CI in external validation, delta C-statistic 0.126 with P, 44 vs 44 matched. Conclusion (about 50 words) restates the claim with promotional wording. Followed by keywords and a boxed "Translational perspectives" paragraph (about 100 words) that summarises clinical meaning.

**Introduction.** Four paragraphs, about 550 words. Funnel: (1) CAD burden, CCTA role, residual risk, inflammation, limits of blood biomarkers and PET; (2) the group's PVAT biology and FAI, prognosis in CRISP-CT, hint of modifiability; (3) "However", structural remodelling is not captured, radiomics and machine learning as the enabler, radiotranscriptomic concept; (4) one-sentence hypothesis ("We hypothesized that comprehensive CCTA-based radiomic phenotyping"). No explicit aim of three studies, no results preview, no contribution list.

**Methods.** Order: Study design (demographics pointer to supplement, workflow Figure 1, ethics and consent in three sentences at the very start); Study 1, Study 2, Study 3 subsections, each opening with a purpose clause ("To understand how fibrosis and vascularity ...", "To train and subsequently validate ...", "To compare the ability of FRP ..."); CCTA protocol and definitions; Radiomic analysis (segmentation, characterization, with software versions and reproducibility ICCs); tissue and gene expression; Statistical analysis organised per study, ending with software and the two-sided alpha. Model rationale is justified in one sentence (random forest handles non-linearity and small data). Much detail is pushed to supplementary material.

**Results.** Subheadings are descriptive phrases tagged with the study ("(Study 1)", "(Study 2a)", "(Study 2b)", "(Study 3)"). Cohort characteristics are not described in text; they sit in supplementary tables. Each paragraph walks through figure panels with call-outs at clause end. Comparisons phrased as "had a 10.8-fold higher risk", "was positively associated with", "no significant changes were observed". Interpretation is not avoided: paragraphs end with inference sentences ("This proof-of-concept analysis suggests", "confirming the cardiac-specific nature of the biomarker", "These findings further support the notion that").

**Discussion.** Unheaded opening paragraph (about 200 words) summarising principal findings across the three studies, starting from context (FAI) then "Using a radiotranscriptomic approach, we now demonstrate", then "Following this discovery". Then thematic subheadings: PVAT as a sensor of vascular biology (prior work, largely self-cited), discovering radiotranscriptomic signatures (comparison with oncology radiomics and plaque radiomics, mechanism), developing and validating FRP, FRP and AMI (clinical implication: FRP for first-time screening, FAI for monitoring). Limitations is a single paragraph (about 260 words) near the end, under its own heading; each limitation is paired with a mitigation or future step (more cohorts, automated software, ongoing validation study). Conclusions (5 sentences) restates the claim at full strength, ends with "In conclusion" and "novel, promising approach".

**Back matter.** Headings in order: Supplementary material, Acknowledgements, Funding, Conflict of interest, References. Ethics sits in Methods. No data availability, code availability, author contributions or reporting summary. Conflicts are detailed (patent numbers, founders of a spin-out company).
**Figures and tables.** Captions start with a short noun-phrase title ("Workflow diagram.", "The pericoronary fat radiomic profile."), then panel-by-panel explanation with bold panel letters in parentheses, then statistical test notes, then an alphabetical abbreviation list separated by semicolons. Abbreviations are redefined in every caption. The Take home figure caption is a narrative paragraph explaining the mechanism. Tables are all supplementary.

# Checklist candidates

- Use a declarative title that names the method, the target, the outcome and the imaging modality. [strong]
- Follow the journal's structured abstract headings and organise the results part by study when the paper has several linked studies. [strong]
- Report the headline performance number with its 95% CI and the incremental metric with its P value in the abstract. [strong]
- End the Introduction with an explicit hypothesis or aim sentence. [strong]
- Open the Methods with a study design subsection that holds the ethics approval and consent statement and points to a workflow figure. [strong]
- Open each study subsection with a purpose clause stating why it was done. [strong]
- Give software names and versions and justify the choice of model in one sentence. [strong]
- Report inter- and intra-observer agreement for measurements with ICC and 95% CI. [strong]
- Present effect sizes as estimate, 95% CI, P value in one bracket, with one consistent CI format. [weak]
- Pair counts with percentages in the form n (x.x%). [strong]
- Tag Results subheadings with the study or analysis they report. [strong]
- Open the Discussion with an unheaded paragraph summarising principal findings. [strong]
- Pair each limitation with its mitigation or a planned validation step. [strong]
- Define each abbreviation once in the abstract and once in the main text, and do not redefine it later in the main text. [weak]
- Use one name for one metric throughout (C-statistic or AUC, not both). [weak]
- Redefine abbreviations in each figure caption as an alphabetical list at the end. [strong]
- Start each caption with a short title phrase, then explain panels in order. [strong]
- Disclose patents and company ownership specifically in the conflict of interest statement. [strong]

# Notable deviations

- Promotional wording in abstract and conclusion ("striking improvement", "state-of-the-art", "AI-powered", "promising"), and commercial plans in Limitations; avoid for npj Digital Medicine and Lancet Digital Health.
- Very long Methods sentences (75 to 80 words) with nested brackets and covariate lists inline.
- Interpretation inside Results paragraphs, against the usual separation of results and discussion.
- A random 80/20 split of one case-control pool is called "external validation"; TRIPOD would call this internal (split-sample) validation.
- No reporting guideline named, and no data or code availability statement; both are required by the target journals.
- Abbreviation hygiene is loose: AI and CRISP-CT redefined, single-use abbreviations (GPU, OXACCT), a caption abbreviation mismatched to its expansion.
- Inconsistent number formats (CI labels, "6" and "six" months, 1575 vs 1,575, AUC decimals).
- Strong certainty ("confirming") attached to a 16-patient subgroup.
- Heavy self-citation for background; no main-text Table 1 (cohort data only in supplement).
